WHEN
  After `underdraw check` answers "ok": true, to read the node boxes and
  know where each part went, at each of the widths the project draws at:
  those its tokens.json names ("widths"), or a phone width (390) and a
  desktop width (1120); with --all, every state at each of them at once.
  What a person looks at is the gallery (`underdraw gallery`), not
  these PNGs.

WHO
  The agent that writes the description.

READS
  <path>/screen.json, its tokens.json (spaces, radii, fonts, breakpoints;
  the nearest at or above <path>, as `underdraw --help` says); each node is
  drawn as the screen's own "components" or underdraw's say. Refuses a
  description `check` finds faults in. With
  --root, also the "screen" of every description under that directory, as
  `check <root>` reads them: a node that navigates to a screen none of them
  declares is assumed, a screen not drawn yet. Without it, navigates are
  not resolved.

WRITES
  One PNG: --out, or <dir>/renders/<fixture or phase>-<width>.png. Running
  it again overwrites the same file.
  With --all, one sheet instead: --out, or <dir>/renders/all.png. A row
  for each state, the fixtures then the phases in the order written (as
  `check` lists them), each drawn at each of the project's widths side
  by side, in the order its tokens.json names them, each
  page captioned with its state and width ("default · 390"). --all takes
  no --fixture, --phase or --width.

ANSWERS
  {
    "png":        the file written
    "project":    the screen's project root: the nearest directory at or
                  above it holding domain.json, or its own directory
    "tokens":     the tokens.json it took, or null when there is none
    "state":      {"fixture": name} or {"phase": name}
    "breakpoint": "compact" or "regular", chosen by tokens' breakpoints and --width
    "width", "height": the page in points; the page is as tall as its content
    "scale":      pixels per point (lowered when the page is taller than the GPU draws)
    "pixels":     [width, height] of the PNG
    "nodes":      [{"id", "component", "box": {"x", "y", "width", "height"} in points,
                    "label": the number tagged on it in the picture, or null,
                    "assumed": what the agent guessed there, or that its component is
                      not one underdraw knows, or (given --root) that it
                      navigates to a screen not drawn yet, or null; what
                      underdraw assumes itself is a whole sentence in
                      --lang, en, zh or ja, en by default}, …]
  }

ANSWERS, WITH --all
  {
    "png", "project", "tokens", "scale", "pixels": as above, for the sheet
    "width", "height": the sheet in points
    "states": [{"state", "breakpoint", "width", "height", "nodes": each as
                  one render of that state at that width answers it, its
                  node boxes in that page's own points,
                "at": {"x", "y"}, where the page's top left sits on the
                  sheet, in points}, …], each state at each width in turn
  }
  A node's place on the sheet is "at" plus its box, times "scale" in
  pixels. A long sheet is drawn at a lower scale, so its pages are
  smaller than one render's; render the one state to read it closely.

WHAT YOU SEE
  A wireframe, in grays: boxes, text in the system font, crossed boxes for
  images. A red numbered circle tags everything a person can act on, in
  document order; the same number is "label" in nodes, so "3" in a
  conversation means one node. An orange dashed outline and "?" mark what
  was assumed, for the person to confirm, and a component underdraw does not
  know, drawn as a box with its name. The "?" sits at the node's top-right
  corner, always wholly on the page; on a node as big as the page (a
  full-screen overlay) it sits at the top right of what the node shows.
  A number sits left of its node's top. Neither covers another node's
  text: where the node beside it has words there, as in a tight row of
  buttons, the tag moves elsewhere by its node (off the corner, above,
  below, or to the right), so a word cut short in the picture is cut short
  in the layout too.
  What a dialog (an opaque box pinned over the page) or its backdrop covers
  gets no number and no "?": its "label" is null, and its "assumed" still
  says why it was guessed. Icons are Material Symbols, drawn outlined, or
  solid for a name ending in .fill.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP
  - Needs a GPU (Metal on macOS); `underdraw doctor` says whether there is one.
  - The page shows everything at once: a sticky bar is drawn at the end of
    the page, not over the content, and a node's "foot" on the bottom edge
    of its column, after all it holds.

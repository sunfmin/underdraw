WHEN
  Whenever the person should see a design: after `underdraw check` answers
  "ok": true, and again after each round of edits. The gallery is what a
  person looks at; `render` is for the agent reading node boxes.

WHO
  The agent that writes descriptions, for the person it works with.

READS
  Every screen description under <root>: each directory, at any depth,
  that holds screen.json. The tree leaves out renders/ and platforms/,
  hidden directories, and directories leading to no description. A
  project's root lists the components it shares in a section of their own,
  components/, each a description (components/<Name>) whose states are the
  fixtures in its components/<Name>.json: one instance of it, drawn in each,
  picked, noted and copied as a screen's nodes are, its notes kept in
  components/<Name>/notes.json (`underdraw check --help`, COMPONENTS). Nothing is
  read from renders/: the window draws from the descriptions themselves,
  from the notes.json beside each, the person's notes, and from each
  journey under <root> (<dir>/journeys/<name>.json).

DOES
  Opens a window in a process of its own and exits at once; only the window
  keeps running, until the person closes it. There is one window per root:
  while it is open, running this again brings it forward instead of opening
  a second, so run it after every round of edits.

  The window has three parts. On the left, the tree. In the middle, what
  is picked is drawn. On the right, the sidebar: a card for
  each thing about what is picked (the node or arrow picked, or the state
  shown, the journey played, what an act came to), each with buttons,
  clicked by their own box, for what can be done with it; under them, the
  note on what is picked, as a conversation; and, fixed at its foot, a box
  to write in and a Send button, to talk to the agent (below). With nothing picked to talk
  about (an overview or a flow, no arrow picked), it says what a click, a
  ⌘-click and ⌘C do. ⌘\ (Ctrl+\ on Linux and Windows) hides the sidebar and shows it
  again; it is shown when the window opens, and while it is hidden the
  pictures take its width. Scroll the sidebar with the wheel over it.
  The tree opens 260pt wide and the sidebar 360pt. Drag the line between
  either and the pictures (the pointer turns into a left-right arrow over
  it) to widen or narrow it: the tree from 160pt, the sidebar from 280pt,
  each up to what leaves the pictures, beside the other, room for the
  phone's at half size. The pictures fit the width left as you drag; names
  too long for the tree are cut at its edge, and the sidebar's cards
  rewrap to its width. A press on a line picks nothing and presses no
  button. While the sidebar is hidden it has no line; shown again, it is
  as wide as it was dragged. The widths are not kept once the window
  closes.

  Each pane that scrolls shows a scrollbar while what it holds is larger
  than it, and none while it fits: the tree and the sidebar on their right
  edge, the pictures on their right edge and, when they scroll sideways,
  along their foot. It stays shown while it is needed, not faded out. Its
  thumb's length is the share in view and its place how far the pane is
  scrolled, and it follows at once as you scroll, resize the window or
  pick something else. It runs clear of the tree's names and the
  sidebar's buttons, and under a picture's labels and orange "?". Scroll
  with the wheel or trackpad, or with the scrollbar itself: drag its
  thumb and the pane scrolls with it, as far as it goes and no further;
  press its track either side of the thumb and the pane scrolls one page
  that way. The thumb darkens under the pointer and while it is dragged.
  A press on a scrollbar only scrolls: it picks no line of the tree and
  no node, and presses no button. A scrollbar keeps clear of
  the lines between the panes, so a press is on one or the other.

  The tree holds the directories and descriptions as they are arranged on disk, under
  a first line for <root> itself when it holds no description (picked, it
  shows the flow and the inbox of everything under it), each
  description opened into its states: its fixtures, then its phases (in
  gray), each in the order written. Picking a state draws it as a wireframe
  at 390 (a phone) and 1120 (a desktop) side by side, labelled as `render`
  labels them. The two pictures scale down together to fit the width left
  between the tree and the sidebar, down to half size; in a window too
  narrow for that they stay at half size and scroll sideways (a trackpad's
  sideways swipe, or Shift and the wheel). Their labels, and the orange
  "?", stay at full size, readable at half size, and a click picks the
  node under it at any size. Picking a description shows its overview: every state, small
  and captioned with its name, in a grid in the order the tree lists them,
  tall ones cut at the foot, and each transition the description declares
  (`transitions` in screen.json) as an arrow from its `from` state to its
  `to`, labelled with the number the node acted on is tagged with in the
  `from` state and, for an event that calls an operation, the outcome
  ("4 · ok"); one that stays in its state loops back into it. A state no
  transition reaches or leaves is still shown. Clicking a state's picture
  picks that state. Picking a directory draws the flow between the screens
  under it: each description a box named as its directory, holding a small picture of its first state
  at 390, and each navigate an arrow from the box of the description it is
  in to the box of the screen it names, labelled with the node's id. The
  arrows are the "links" `underdraw check <root>` reports, no other: a
  navigate to a description outside the directory brings that box in too;
  one to a screen no description under <root> declares, a screen not drawn
  yet, ends at a dashed orange box marked "?" and named as the screen it
  asks for, one for each such screen. A description with faults is a box
  marked faulty, with how many, and no picture. Clicking a description's
  box picks that description in the tree, showing its overview. Scroll a tall
  page with the mouse wheel or trackpad. Clicking a node in the picture
  picks the innermost there, an overlay (a badge, a dialog, a sheet) before
  what it floats over, outlines it at both widths, and shows its card in
  the sidebar: what it is (its component, as the title), the operation it
  calls when acted on ("calls"), and what was assumed there (`assumed`).
  Its id, and its label and box at each width, as `render` reports them,
  are folded into "▸ details": click it to unfold them, and again to fold
  them, for every node picked after. A node that navigates to a screen no description under
  <root> declares is drawn with the orange "?", a screen not drawn yet, as
  `underdraw check <root>` reports it. Every word the window says itself
  is said in --lang, en, zh or ja, en by default: the sidebar, the tree's
  marks, a description's faults heading, the flow's and the overview's
  marks, the window's title, and what underdraw assumes itself. With ja
  those words are set in Japanese fonts, whose Han characters take the
  Japanese forms. Only what
  the files say stays as written: ids, names, states, notes and findings.
  Picking another state keeps that id picked while the state has it;
  clicking where no node is closes its card. A description with
  faults stays in the tree, marked with how many it has; picking it, or any
  of its states, lists each finding as `underdraw check` names it (where,
  then what is wrong) instead of a picture. Fix them and check it again.

  Play a journey: each <dir>/journeys/<name>.json is listed in the tree
  under <dir>, after its states when <dir> is a description, as
  "→ <name>" (`underdraw check --help`, FORMAT, says how one is written).
  Picking one plays it from its first step: the step's state drawn at
  both widths, as a state picked is, and the node the person acts on next
  ringed twice in blue at each width that shows it (a repeat's first
  instance). The sidebar says which step of how many, the step's
  description and state, and the act ("acts on order-{index} (label 3) ·
  tap, answered ok"; "the journey ends here" on the last). Step forward
  with → (or "→ next step" in the sidebar) and back with ← (or "← previous
  step"). The journey stays picked as it plays, and the step shown stays
  as the files change. ⌘-click acts on a node from the step's state, as
  the prototype does, leaving the journey; picking another line in the
  tree stops it. A journey `underdraw check <root>` finds broken is marked
  in the tree with how many faults it has; picking it lists each (the
  step where it breaks, then what is wrong) instead of playing it.

  An overview marks each state with the journeys that pass through it, so
  a change to a screen shows whose paths it lies on: a blue mark over the
  state's picture, at its top left, for each step of a journey listed in
  the tree that is in that state ("→ cancel-an-order · step 2"), stacked
  when there are several. Clicking a mark plays that journey from that
  step; one that is broken shows its faults instead, as picking it does.

  Click through a design as a prototype: a plain click inspects a node, as
  above; hold ⌘ (Ctrl on Linux and Windows) and click to act on it. Acting
  raises the node's tap (or its first event, when it has no tap) and
  follows what the description says happens there:
  - a transition declared from the state shown on that event shows its
    `to` state. Where the event calls an operation and the transitions
    name more than one outcome, the sidebar lists them (`ok → default`,
    `shipped → cancel-ready`): click the one it answered with.
  - a `navigate` shows the first state of the description under <root>
    that declares that screen; to a screen no description declares, the
    sidebar says it is not drawn yet.
  - `back` returns along the path taken by acting, across screens, one
    step each time. Picking a state in the tree starts a new path.
  - nothing declared: the screen stays, and the sidebar says so ("no
    transition from <state> on <node id as written>.tap · should go to:"),
    then lists the description's states and, as "navigate to <screen>",
    the screens the other descriptions under <root> declare. Click one to
    propose it: the window writes a note on the node holding the proposal
    (`underdraw notes --help`), and the agent declares it, in the
    description's `transitions` (`underdraw check --help`, FORMAT) or as
    a `navigate`. The window still never writes the description.
  The sidebar says what each act came to, in a card above the node's.

  A drag over a picture picks nothing more than the press it started
  with: there are no regions. What a part of a state holds is said in the
  state's note.

  Pick an arrow by clicking its label: a transition's in an overview, a
  navigate's (the node's id) in a flow. It is drawn in the strong ink, and
  the sidebar says what it is. Click where no arrow is to drop it.

  Copy a reference to say where, in the conversation: ⌘C (Ctrl+C on Linux
  and Windows) puts one line on the clipboard naming the node picked as
  the description writes it, or, with none picked (click a picture's caption, or where no node is), the state
  shown, or the arrow picked; the sidebar says "copied" and the line. For a
  node:
    <description dir> · fixture <name> · <id as written> at <JSON Pointer> in screen.json · pointed at [<i>], label <n>, width <w>
  e.g. /work/drafts/orders · fixture default · order-{index}-cancel at
  /root/children/2/children/0/children/1/item/children in screen.json ·
  pointed at [2], label 7, width 390. For a state: `<description dir> · phase loading`.
  The id is the one written, a template inside a repeat (`order-{index}`),
  never the id one row was given; the JSON Pointer (RFC 6901) is where that
  node is written in <description dir>/screen.json, under /root, a phase
  (/phases/<name>) or a component (/components/<name>/nodes): edit the node
  there. What follows "pointed at" is context, the one instance the person
  pointed at: the element of each repeat around it ([2] is the third,
  outermost first), its label and the width it was drawn at, which say
  which data showed what they mean; it is not where to edit. For an arrow,
  with the keys screen.json's `transitions`, and the "links" of `underdraw
  check <root>`, name it by:
    <description dir> · transition from <state> on <node id as written>.<event> outcome <outcome> to <state>
    <description dir> · navigate from <screen> on <node id as written> to <screen>
  "outcome" only for an event that calls an operation; a navigate's
  <description dir> is the description it is written in.

  Talk to the agent about what you picked, in the sidebar; nothing needs
  holding to speak. Each of these has one note, a conversation kept in
  notes.json:
  - a node: click it in a state's picture;
  - a whole state: with no node picked, the state shown (click a
    picture's caption, or where no node is, or a state in an overview);
  - a transition: click its arrow's label in an overview;
  - a navigate: click its arrow's label in a flow.
  The sidebar shows the thing's card at the top (a node's details folded
  as they are; for a state, "<state> · the whole state"), then its note
  as a conversation, scrolling with the cards, newest at the bottom: its
  id and where it stands in its title (waiting on the agent while you
  spoke last, answered once the agent did), then what you wrote, in blue
  bubbles on the right, and what the agent said, in bubbles outlined green
  on the left, in the order said ("note · nothing said yet" before
  anything is). Under your last message, while it waits on the agent,
  a gray line says whether the agent has it: "waiting for the agent"
  until `underdraw notes --wait` hands it over, then "the agent is
  changing it (23s)", counting up each second while the window is open,
  until the agent answers. A count that keeps growing says the agent may
  have stopped: start it again. An answer is unread until its
  conversation is shown in the sidebar; shown, it is read, and stays read
  when the window is opened again. A node's note runs on whichever instance of it is picked,
  and holds the notes that answer its assumption or propose where acting
  on it goes. At the sidebar's foot, fixed there while the cards scroll, a
  box to write in and a Send button under it: Enter or Send sends what the
  box holds, Shift+Enter breaks the line. While the box is empty, Send is
  gray and Enter does nothing. Sending adds what you wrote to the thing's
  note in notes.json, starting the note when it has none, and shows it in
  the conversation at once, scrolled to; the box empties. What is typed
  stays in the box as you pick something else, and is sent to what is
  picked when you send it.
  The box is edited as a text field: ← → ↑ ↓, Home and End move the
  caret (↑ and ↓ by the lines it shows, Home and End to either end of
  one); typing goes in where it is, Backspace and Delete take the
  character before or after it. Shift with those keys, or a drag over the
  text, selects; a click puts the caret there. Typing or pasting replaces
  what is selected, Backspace and Delete take it out. ⌘A selects it all,
  ⌘C copies what is selected (with nothing selected, the reference, as
  above), ⌘X cuts it, ⌘V pastes (Ctrl on Linux and Windows). While a
  journey plays, ← and → step it while the box is empty, and move the
  caret once it holds something. What an input method composes (pinyin,
  before it becomes 汉字) is shown underlined at the caret, its
  candidates beside it, and only what it commits is typed: Enter and the
  other keys are its own while it composes, so the Enter that confirms a
  candidate sends nothing. Esc lets go of the node or arrow picked. While
  a ✗ is written (below), its field takes the keys and the box is not
  shown.
  A note is kept in notes.json beside the description's screen.json (a
  navigate's, beside the one it is written in), pinned as ⌘C references
  it: the node as written, the state, and the instance pointed at; the
  state; the transition; or the navigate. A pin numbered N1, N2… is
  drawn on it, in where the note stands: blue while it waits on the
  agent, green while the agent's answer is unread, a faint gray once it
  is answered and read: at a node's top-right corner, in the state and at the width
  it was written in, every other state and width that holds that node
  showing a faint dot there; at the right of a state's caption, in its
  pictures and in the overview; beside a transition's label; under a
  navigate's arrow, where it leaves its box. The pins of the conversation
  shown are ringed. Click a pin to pick what its note is on, and so show
  its conversation. The tree shows how many notes wait on the agent on
  each description and on each state ("2 notes"): a note on an arrow
  counts on its description.
  A note is a conversation, never closed. The agent reads them with
  `underdraw notes <root>` and answers one, at any length: its pin turns
  green until you read it, and the pointer over it shows what the agent
  said last. A note
  whose node or arrow is gone (removed or renamed) joins the note
  on its whole state, after a gray line saying where it was ("on `price`,
  now gone"): carry on there. A note written on a region, before regions
  went, is read as on its whole state.

  Answer an assumption: click its orange "?" (or the node it is on). The
  sidebar shows, in a card of its own, what was guessed, in large type,
  what else it could be below it ("or else", what the assumption says
  after its first ";"), and two buttons:
  - [✓ right, keep it]: the guess was right. It is written to notes.json
    only after 5 seconds, in which the sidebar counts down and [undo]
    takes it back with nothing written, so an agent waiting with
    `underdraw notes --wait` never acts on an answer taken back; closing
    the window within them writes it at once. The note says so; the agent
    takes the guess out of the description (a screen not drawn yet: draws
    it), and answers it.
  - [✗ wrong…]: opens a focused field in place of the buttons, for what
    is right; Enter sends it as a note on the node, and it is not sent
    empty. The agent changes the description and answers it, as any note.
  The sidebar lists the notes that answer it already ("N3 answers it ✗
  wrong · answered"). The window writes
  notes.json, and only the person's acts; it never writes a description:
  the agent makes every change. Commit notes.json with the description.

  The inbox: with a description, one of its states, or a directory (the
  root's line too) picked, and no node or arrow, the sidebar lists
  what waits on you there, in a card "waits on you · <how many>", one box
  an item: each assumption no note answers yet, marked with an orange "?"
  ("<node id as written> in <state>", what was guessed under it), then each
  note the agent has answered that you have not read, marked with its
  blue id ("N2", what it is on, and "agent: <what it said last>" under it
  in green), for you to read; once read, it leaves. A directory's lists every description under it, each under its
  directory's name, in the order the tree lists them. The notes you spoke
  last in, waiting on the agent, are folded to how many there are
  ("▸ waiting on the agent · 2"); click it to list them, each a box marked
  with its id, and again to fold them. Click an item to show its state with what
  it is on picked, the node or arrow (a transition's in the overview, a
  navigate's in the flow of the directory above it), and its card: the
  node's, to answer the assumption with ✓ or ✗, and the conversation, to
  read it. The inbox gives way to the card of a node or arrow picked;
  click where no node is to see it again, under the state's conversation. It is drawn from
  the files as they now stand: an assumption answered, or taken out of the
  description, and a note answered or said more in, leave it or
  move within it as they change.

  The window watches <root> and is drawn from the files as they now stand:
  edit a description's screen.json or notes.json (`underdraw
  notes --answer` does), or a tokens.json under <root> it takes its tokens
  from, and the state shown is drawn again within a moment, scrolled where
  it was and with the node picked still picked while it is there, without
  the window coming forward; a description that gains faults shows them,
  and its picture again once they are fixed. A description directory added
  under <root> comes into the tree, and one removed leaves it; so does a
  journey, and one edited is checked and played again. When the
  state shown goes, its description's overview is shown instead. The
  window opens on the first description's overview.

ANSWERS
  {
    "root":         the directory searched
    "descriptions": the directories holding a screen.json, relative to root,
                    in the order the tree lists them
    "faults":       those of them `underdraw check` finds faults in, in the
                    same order; [] when every one can be drawn. Run
                    `underdraw check <root>/<each>` to read and fix them
    "opened":       true: a window was opened; false: the window over this
                    root was already open, and is brought forward
    "pid":          the window's process
  }
  Exits 2 with {"error"} when there is no description under <root>, or no
  display to open a window on (over SSH, in CI): there, `underdraw render`
  draws a state to a PNG instead.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP
  - Needs a display and a GPU; checked on macOS only, so far.
  - The window is the root's once the path is resolved: `drafts` and
    `./drafts/` are one root; `drafts` and `drafts/todo` are two windows.
